# Friction Audit — Sessionize, and where it leaves off

**Method:** Reviewed sessionize.com, its Features and For-Speakers pages, and the
Sessionize Playbook (platform overview, schedule builder, submission and editing
guides). Sessionize is the reference and the competitor. The goal is to find the
specific places it creates friction or simply stops — those gaps are the opening
for **Lectern**, the app built in Run 1.

Sessionize does five core jobs well: (1) organizers spin up a Call for Speakers
form fast, (2) speakers submit sessions and a bio, (3) a content team votes to
select, (4) organizers drag accepted sessions onto a multi-room/multi-day
schedule, and (5) a public event page is published. It is genuinely good at the
top of the funnel. It is also where it stops.

## The seven friction points

1. **Repetitive re-entry for speakers.** Sessionize keeps a default speaker
   profile, but per its own Playbook, editing your tagline/bio does *not*
   propagate to events you've already submitted to — you re-edit per event. Bio,
   headshot, links, and pitch get re-entered or re-tuned submission after
   submission. For frequent speakers this is repetitive busywork; for a first-timer
   it is a long, intimidating wall of fields.

2. **One long form, no chunking, no calm progress.** The submission is a single
   dense form with however many custom fields the organizer added. There is no
   step-by-step breakdown, no calm progress indicator, and no save-and-resume
   designed into the speaker flow. For a neurodiverse submitter (ADHD especially),
   a long undifferentiated form is exactly the pattern that causes abandonment —
   and abandoned submissions are lost content for the organizer.

3. **Selection is entirely manual human voting.** Review = invite a team, vote,
   read statistics. There is no first-pass triage: no auto-summary, no fit/quality
   signal, no duplicate or off-topic flag. On a CFP with hundreds of submissions,
   organizers read everything cold. The platform gives them a voting booth, not a
   co-pilot.

4. **Desktop-first, drag-heavy interactions.** The schedule builder is a
   drag-and-drop grid; submission and review assume a large screen. Speakers
   increasingly start on a phone, and a drag-grid is hard to operate by keyboard or
   screen reader. Mobile and assistive-tech parity are clearly secondary.

5. **Accessibility is not a stated commitment.** The only inclusion feature
   surfaced is multi-language session/bio support. There is no published WCAG
   2.2 / ADA / Section 508 stance, no reduced-motion or high-contrast controls, no
   focus mode. In K-12 and higher-ed procurement, an accessibility conformance
   posture (a VPAT, a WCAG claim) is a gating requirement — its absence is a
   disqualifier before features are even compared.

6. **The record ends at "the event happened."** Sessionize's own overview frames
   it as call-for-papers + schedule + speaker management. There is no attendee
   roster it owns, no registration, no check-in/attendance capture. The data it
   holds is about *content and speakers*, not *who showed up* — so it cannot be the
   record an institution needs after the doors close.

7. **No continuing-education layer at all — the big gap.** There is no
   credit-hour tracking, no attendance-to-credit rules, and no certificate
   generation (completion or participation). For the entire professional-development
   world — where the point of attending is the clock hours / CEUs on an educator's
   license — this is the part that actually matters, and it is done in spreadsheets
   and mail-merge outside the platform every single time.

## Where Sessionize leaves off (the wedge)

```
  Sessionize covers:                          Lectern continues:
  CFP → submit → review → schedule → public │ register → attend/check-in →
                                            │ accrue CE credit → issue
                                            │ certificate → multi-event record
  ─────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────
                                            ↑
                              Sessionize stops here.
                       Everything to the right is compliance-driven,
                       spreadsheet-trapped, and becomes the customer's
                       system of record — which is why it is the wedge.
```

Sessionize selects talks and builds a schedule. **Lectern owns the full lifecycle
from call-for-papers to certified credit**, because the roster, attendance, credit
hours, and certificates are the records an education institution is legally and
operationally required to keep — and once those live in Lectern, the institution
cannot easily leave.

**Accessibility assessment.** Sessionize publishes no WCAG/ADA/508 conformance
posture and ships no user-facing accessibility controls. Lectern targets WCAG 2.2
AA and aligns to ADA / Section 508 as a structural property of every flow, with
the speaker submission experience as the proof point and user-controllable
reduced-motion / text-size / high-contrast / focus-mode preferences as a
differentiator. In the beachhead (K-12 and higher-ed PD) this is not polish — it
is the procurement gate that opens the market.
